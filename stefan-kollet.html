Research

The terrestrial system as one connected water and energy cycle.

Groundwater, soil moisture, rivers, vegetation and the atmosphere are usually studied apart. My work treats them as one coupled system: lateral groundwater flow shapes soil moisture, which shapes evapotranspiration, which feeds back on the boundary layer and precipitation.

To capture these feedbacks at the scales where they matter, we develop open-source simulation platforms that run from a laptop to the largest supercomputers and, increasingly, on GPUs.

Integrated hydrologic model

ParFlow

A parallel, integrated hydrology model simulating spatially distributed surface and subsurface flow together with land surface processes such as evapotranspiration and snow. I am one of its core developers.

  • Richards’ equation implicitly coupled to shallow-water overland flow
  • Terrain-following grid from the land surface to deep aquifers
  • Scales to very large core counts and runs on GPUs

Regional Earth system model

TSMP

The Terrestrial Systems Modelling Platform couples atmosphere, land surface and subsurface into one scale-consistent, modular, massively parallel regional Earth system model.

  • Mass, energy and momentum exchange across compartments
  • From catchment to continental domains
  • Built around ParFlow, a land surface model and an atmospheric model

From research to decisions

Water resources forecasts for central Europe.

Since 2020, ParFlow with its integrated land surface model has run in an experimental, quasi-operational setup over central Europe on GPUs. Ensemble forecasts driven by ECMWF predictions feed an experimental Water Resources Bulletin that maps subsurface water storage anomalies — including shallow groundwater — against a long-term model climatology.

The same system was used to analyse forecasts and hindcasts of the July 2021 flood in western Germany and eastern Belgium.
